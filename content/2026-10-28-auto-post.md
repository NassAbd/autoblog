## 2. The VC Windfall: A Quick Math Check  

Let’s illustrate the VC upside with a simple calculation. Assume a VC fund invested $200 M in the Series C round at a $4 B pre‑money valuation (post‑money $4.5 B). The IPO values the company at $15 B, and the fund retains its proportional ownership (ignoring dilution for simplicity).

```python
# Simple VC return calculator
investment = 200_000_000          # $200M
post_money_valuation = 4_500_000_000
ownership_pct = investment / post_money_valuation

ipo_valuation = 15_000_000_000
exit_value = ownership_pct * ipo_valuation

multiple = exit_value / investment
print(f"Ownership: {ownership_pct:.2%}")
print(f"Exit value: ${exit_value/1e9:.2f}B")
print(f"Multiple: {multiple:.1f}x")
```

**Output**

```
Ownership: 4.44%
Exit value: $0.67B
Multiple: 3.3x
```

A 3.3‑times return on a $200 M stake translates to **$660 M**—a tidy profit, but not a *billions‑of‑dollars* payday for a single fund. Across the entire VC ecosystem, the aggregate returns could indeed reach the “billions” figure cited by the press, but the headline number masks a more nuanced reality: **the bulk of future cash flow will come from the Amazon‑Anthropic partnership, not from the one‑off IPO proceeds**.
